The 4 Core Pillars of OOP
To fully understand OOP, look at how the car example applies its four main principles:
Encapsulation: The internal wiring of the car engine is hidden under the hood. You only interact with the steering wheel and pedals (the public interface).
Inheritance: A specific type of car, like an ElectricCar, can inherit all features of the base Car class but add its own unique attributes like BatteryLife.
Polymorphism: A startEngine() method might work differently for a GasCar (cranking an engine) versus an ElectricCar (silently powering up), even though they share the same command name.
Abstraction: You can drive the car without needing to understand the complex thermodynamics happening inside the engine block.


A Class is a blueprint, and an Object is an instance of that class.

You can implement encapsulation in Java by following two standard steps:
Declare variables as private: This hides the data from direct external access.
Provide public getter and setter methods: This allows safe, controlled access to read and update the values.
